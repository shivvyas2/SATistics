Chapter nine

9The games, and adding one

Six games are registered in frontend/lib/games.ts. They were written at different times and it shows: Zombie, Subway Surfers and Squid Game share a modern shape with phases, feedback, retries and a review list; Pac-Man sits in between; Whack-A-Mole and Balloon Pop are the originals and do the least. This chapter walks through each one as it is today, then gives the steps, the contract and a checklist for adding a seventh.

Read Chapter 8 first. It describes the container pattern, the shared exam UI and the question and save paths that every game here uses. This chapter only covers what each game does differently. Every game renders with Three.js on a WebGL canvas; none of them uses the 2D canvas path in GameRenderer.

9.2Zombie Apocalypse

frontend/games/zombie/ZombieGame.ts          engine, does not extend BaseGame
frontend/games/zombie/types.ts               ZombieConfig, ZombieHudState, ZombieFeedback, ZombieReviewItem
frontend/components/ZombieGameContainer.tsx  default export, arcade skin

A first-person shooter in a graveyard. One zombie walks toward the player for each answer choice, each carrying a sign texture with its letter in LANE_COLORS. The player aims with the pointer and clicks or taps the zombie wearing the right answer; keys 1 to 5 shoot by letter through shootZombie(lane). shootAt(x, y) raycasts from the camera into each walking zombie's hitMeshes.

The horde's speed is (ARRIVE_Z - SPAWN_Z) / secondsPerQuestion, so the zombies reach the player exactly when the 35-second clock runs out, which resolves the question as a miss. A wrong shot or a timeout costs one of MAX_HEALTH (4) hearts; a wrong zombie only flinches and keeps walking. Points are DIFFICULTY_POINTS (100, 150 or 200) plus up to 50 for time left plus 10 per streak step up to 5, multiplied by HINT_POINTS_FACTOR (0.6) for each zombie a hint removed. A first miss is pushed onto the back of the queue and totalQuestions grows by one, as long as the player still has health. The run ends when the queue is empty or health reaches zero, and onGameOver reports survived as health > 0.

This is the only game in the arcade skin: ArcadeFrame, ArcadeTopBar and ArcadeStartScreen. Sounds are synthesized with an AudioContext created in start(), so they begin after a user gesture. The scene is all primitives. Limits: the gun and view follow the pointer, which a touch player only moves by tapping.

9.3Whack-A-Mole

frontend/games/whackamole/WhackAMoleGame.ts    extends BaseGame
frontend/games/whackamole/types.ts             SATQuestion (legacy), GameAnalytics, QuestionAttempt
frontend/games/whackamole/questions.ts         satQuestions fallback set
frontend/components/WhackAMoleGameContainer.tsx

Four moles rise from a grass board, labelled A to D. The question and its four choices are shown in DOM panels above and below the board, colour-matched to the moles through MOLE_COLORS. The player clicks a mole; the engine raycasts in its own click listener and calls submitAnswer(index). If nobody is hit within popDuration (QUICK_SECONDS_PER_QUESTION * 1000), it submits -1. A right answer scores 100 + streak * 10. After every answer a 2-second setTimeout shows the next question, and the game ends when all questions have been asked.

Limits worth knowing. The game asks every question it was given, which is the 50 requested from fetchQuestionsWithCache, not the player's chosen count. It always builds four moles, so a fifth choice cannot be picked. There is no right-or-wrong feedback in the UI: the container keeps selectedAnswer and showResult state that nothing renders. The loop does not cap deltaTime. cleanup() removes listeners created with a fresh .bind(this), which never matches the ones added, so they are not actually removed. The pending setTimeout is not cancelled on pause or unmount.

9.4Balloon Pop

frontend/games/carnival/CarnivalGame.ts      extends BaseGame
frontend/games/carnival/types.ts             CarnivalGameState with bulletsRemaining
frontend/games/carnival/questions.ts         a near copy of whackamole/questions.ts
frontend/components/CarnivalGameContainer.tsx

The id is carnival; the catalogue calls it Balloon Pop. Four balloons bob in front of the player, labelled A to D. Each click fires a visible bullet from the camera and spends one of three darts; hitting a balloon pops it with a particle burst and submits that answer. Running out of darts without a hit submits -1. Scoring, the 2-second gap and the end condition are the same as Whack-A-Mole, and so are the four-choice limit, the 50-question fetch and the listener leak. The container shows a single strategyHint behind a Hint button and draws the dart count with Pips.

There is no clock, so a player can think as long as they like. Balloon motion and the pop and particle animations advance per frame rather than per second, and the latter two run in their own requestAnimationFrame chains that ignore pause.

9.5Subway Surfers

frontend/games/subway-surfers/SubwaySurfersGame.ts   extends BaseGame, about 1,300 lines
frontend/games/subway-surfers/types/game.ts          Game, LANE_COLORS, LANE_LETTERS, Runner* types
frontend/components/SubwaySurfersGameContainer.tsx
frontend/public/games/subway-surfers/assets/subway_surfers_maps/scene.gltf
frontend/public/Flying.fbx, Falling.fbx, Falling Flat Impact.fbx
frontend/public/bg-music.mp3, falling.mp3, dying.mp3

A flying runner over an endless subway map. Each question spawns one gate per choice across the lanes. The player steers with the arrow keys, A and D, number keys or a swipe; the gates close in as the question's time runs out, and the answer is whatever lane the runner is in when it reaches the gate. Pressing up, W, Space or Enter calls dive(), which speeds the runner to DIVE_SPEED to commit early. Hints call eliminateLane, which hides that gate and moves the runner off it; moveLane skips eliminated lanes.

The run is shaped like the digital SAT. The constructor shuffles the pool and splits questionCount into two modules. Module 1 is an even mix of difficulties. If the player got at least HARDER_MODULE_ACCURACY (60%) of it right, module 2 prefers hard questions, otherwise easy ones, and onModuleChange tells the container which, so it can explain during the 4-second break. Each module has a section clock of secondsPerQuestion times its length; a single gate waits at most QUESTION_TIME_CAP (1.5) times the per-question budget. When the section clock runs out, the questions left are recorded as unanswered. A first miss is appended to the module and adds 35 seconds to its clock. A wrong answer plays the falling and impact animations before the next question.

The map is MAP_TILE_COUNT (3) copies of the glTF recycled ahead of the runner, with obstacles randomized per tile and trains moving on some tracks. Every REBASE_DISTANCE (3,000 units) the world is shifted back toward the origin to keep floating-point precision. Limits: it does not record a local high score, and frontend/public/games/subway-surfers/jake_subway_surfers/ is shipped but not loaded by any code.

9.6Squid Game

frontend/games/squid-game/SquidGameGame.ts   extends BaseGame, about 1,150 lines
frontend/games/squid-game/types.ts           SquidPhase, SquidOutcome, SquidHudState, ...
frontend/games/squid-game/yuka.d.ts          type stub for the unused yuka package
frontend/games/squid-game/questions.json     not imported anywhere
frontend/games/squid-game/assets/            a 72 MB glTF room, not served, not loaded
frontend/components/SquidGameContainer.tsx
frontend/public/games/squid-game/assets/     FBX animations, glTF guard, doll, tree, textures
frontend/public/games/squid-game/audio/      Cocoma.mp3, Redlight.mp3 (GreenLight.mp3 unused)

Red Light, Green Light, and the only deep game: gamePace returns 'deep' for it, so the backend sends passage and multi-step questions, and the clock is the real exam's secondsPerQuestion. The phases are loading, ready, green, turning, question, feedback and done. On green the player runs with W A S D or the arrows (on-screen buttons on phones). When the doll turns, anyone moving faster than CAUGHT_SPEED is shot by the guards; the player loses a life. Then a question appears and one answer pad per choice is laid on the sand ahead. Standing on a pad for PAD_LOCK_SECONDS (2.5) locks it in, or the player presses Space or the lock button for lockIn().

A right answer earns a 4-second green light and steps difficulty up; a wrong one costs a life, earns 1.5 seconds and steps difficulty down. beginQuestion picks the unused question closest to the current difficulty, and returns a missed question after one other. The run speed is computed in the constructor so a player at EXPECTED_ACCURACY (70%) reaches the finish line about when the questions run out. Every run starts with STARTING_LIVES, five lives. Outcomes are victory (crossed the finish, plus FINISH_BONUS 500 and 100 per life), eliminated and short (out of questions first). Twenty-four NPC_COUNT contestants are cloned skinned meshes that occasionally fail to stop. The calculator opens by default for quant.

Limits: the six models load in parallel through track(), and the 43 MB guard model dominates first-load time. Every hint calls registerHint(), including the first strategy tip, so Squid Game penalizes a tip that Zombie and Subway Surfers give for free. GAME_META says "five misses and you're out", which is only true for some question counts.

9.7Pac-Man

frontend/games/pac-man/PacManGame.ts         extends BaseGame; exports PacManHudState, PacManReviewItem
frontend/components/PacManGameContainer.tsx

A 15 by 15 maze from a hard-coded grid in generateMaze, with dots, power pellets and four ghosts, viewed from above. Every DOTS_PER_QUESTION (10) dots, outside a 5-second cooldown, a question appears: the ghosts freeze, and one lettered answer pellet per choice drops at least MIN_ANSWER_DISTANCE (6) from Pac-Man. Eating a pellet answers. A right answer scores 100 and turns the ghosts blue for 5 seconds; a wrong answer or a timeout costs one of three lives. Hints call eliminateAnswer. Clearing every dot calls nextLevel, which refills the maze and speeds up the ghosts.

Questions come from setQuestions rather than the constructor and repeat in order once the 20 fetched run out. The game only ends when lives reach zero, so the question count preference is ignored. There is no feedback after an answer and no retry queue; QuestionCard is given feedback={null} and totalQuestions={0}. Movement is a fixed moveSpeed of 0.1 per frame, so Pac-Man runs twice as fast on a 120 Hz display, and emitHud() fires every frame.

9.8Mario, the one that is not there

frontend/games/mario/MarioGame.ts extends BaseGame and draws "Mario / Coming Soon..." on a 2D context; its methods are TODO comments and its assets/ folder is empty. GameRenderer imports it and is the only thing that does. It is not in games, has no GAME_META entry, no cover and no container, and /games/mario returns 404. Treat MarioGame, GameRenderer and the canvas branch of GameContainer as dead code. components/GameCard.tsx is also unused.

9.9Adding a new game

Copy the Zombie shape, not the Whack-A-Mole shape. The steps below use a hypothetical id comet; substitute your own, in kebab case, because it becomes the URL, the analytics gameId, the high score key arcade_hi_comet and the game_id column of every saved session.

1. The engine

Create frontend/games/comet/types.ts with a phase union, a config of { questionCount, secondsPerQuestion }, a HUD state, a feedback type and a review item (reuse the shape of ZombieReviewItem so ReviewList accepts it). Then create frontend/games/comet/CometGame.ts. Extending BaseGame is optional; Zombie does not. The skeleton the containers expect is: 

export class CometGame {
  public onHudChange?: (state: CometHudState) => void
  public onQuestionChange?: (question: SATQuestion | null) => void
  public onFeedback?: (feedback: CometFeedback | null) => void
  public onGameOver?: (analytics: GameAnalytics, review: CometReviewItem[]) => void

  constructor(width: number, height: number, canvas: HTMLCanvasElement,
              questions: SATQuestion[], private config: CometConfig) { }

  start(): void { }                       // ready to first question
  update(deltaTime: number): void { }     // milliseconds, already capped
  render(): void { }
  resize(width: number, height: number): void { }
  setViewShift(x: number, y = 0): void { }
  setPaused(paused: boolean): void { }
  setMuted(muted: boolean): void { }
  skipFeedback(): void { }
  eliminateOption(option: number): void { }   // for hints
  cleanup(): void { }                     // dispose geometry, materials, renderer, audio
}

Import SATQuestion from @/lib/api/questions, not from a game folder, and GameAnalytics and QuestionAttempt from @/games/whackamole/types. Use LANE_COLORS and LANE_LETTERS so your targets match the chips in QuestionCard.

2. The container

Create frontend/components/CometGameContainer.tsx as a named export. Follow ZombieGameContainer: load prefs and questions on mount with a cancel flag; await import('@/games/comet/CometGame') in a second effect; assign the callbacks; run the loop with Math.min(currentTime - lastTime, 50); clean up everything in the effect's return. Render GameLoading, then GameIntro, then a HUD (GameTopBar or ArcadeTopBar), a panel with QuestionCard and HintButton measured by useViewShift, a Calculator when prefs.section === 'quant', PauseMenu, and finally GameOverModal with ReviewList as its child. In onGameOver, call recordHighScore and then apiClient.saveScore('comet', analytics) inside a try and catch.

3. Registration

  • frontend/lib/games.ts: append { id: 'comet', name, description, status: 'available' }. This alone makes /games/comet resolve and adds the static param.
  • frontend/lib/gameMeta.ts: add comet: { title, genre, answerBy }. The genre must be a member of Genre; if you add a new genre, add it to GENRES too or it gets no filter chip.
  • frontend/components/GameContainer.tsx: import the container, add 'comet' to OWN_CONTAINER_GAMES, and add an if (game.id === 'comet') return next to the others, after the hook.
  • frontend/components/brand/GameCover.tsx: add a comet entry to COVERS with a bg and SVG art drawn on a 300 by 400 viewBox in the palette constants. Without it the catalogue shows the lime-circle FALLBACK.
  • frontend/lib/exam.ts: add the id to DEEP_GAMES only if the game gives the player time for passage questions; pass the matching seconds per question.
  • frontend/lib/arcade.ts needs no edit; its functions take the id.
  • Assets go under frontend/public/games/comet/ and are loaded by absolute path, for example /games/comet/model.gltf, with loading progress reported through the HUD.

The backend needs nothing. /api/games/save-score accepts any gameId string and the stats endpoints group by it.

The contract

directionwhatrule
inSATQuestion[]Build one target per options entry, two to five. Never assume four.
inconfigquestionCount from prefs; secondsPerQuestion from QUICK_SECONDS_PER_QUESTION or EXAMS.
indeltaTimeMilliseconds. Scale all motion and clocks by it; no setTimeout for game time.
outonQuestionChangeThe question on screen, or null between questions.
outonFeedback{ isCorrect, selected, correctAnswer, points, willRetry }; null to clear.
outonHudChangeA fresh object, throttled to about 10 per second unless forced.
outonGameOverCalled once. gameId equals the registry id; averageResponseTime and every timeSpent are integers.
Integers only. The backend's GameAnalytics declares timeSpent and averageResponseTime as int. Wrap both in Math.round, as Zombie does, or the save can be rejected.

Checklist

  1. Engine in frontend/games/<id>/, no React imports, a cleanup() that disposes everything.
  2. Container in frontend/components/, engine imported dynamically.
  3. Entry in games.ts, gameMeta.ts, GameContainer.tsx (both the list and the return) and GameCover.tsx.
  4. Variable option count handled, including five.
  5. Keyboard and pointer input both work; canvas has touch-none if it takes gestures.
  6. Pause stops every clock; P and Escape open PauseMenu.
  7. Hints remove a target in the scene; a first miss returns later with willRetry.
  8. onGameOver fires once, the review list is passed to ReviewList, the high score is recorded and saveScore is called.
  9. Assets in frontend/public/games/<id>/, sized with the 129 MB already there in mind.
  10. Played once signed in, then checked on the catalogue's "Your games" panel.

9.10Study notes

Read in this order

  1. frontend/games/zombie/ZombieGame.ts, the smallest modern engine.
  2. frontend/components/ZombieGameContainer.tsx, its container.
  3. frontend/games/subway-surfers/SubwaySurfersGame.ts, beginModule to resolveAnswer.
  4. frontend/games/squid-game/SquidGameGame.ts, beginQuestion and updatePads.
  5. frontend/games/whackamole/WhackAMoleGame.ts, to see what not to copy.

Check yourself

Why does Subway Surfers fetch twice the question count?

Module 2 picks harder or easier questions depending on module 1, so the pool must hold more than the run will use. Squid Game does the same so its difficulty can step up and down.

A GRE question has five choices. Which games can a player answer it in?

Zombie, Subway Surfers, Squid Game and Pac-Man, which build one target per option. Whack-A-Mole and Balloon Pop always build four.

You registered a game in games.ts and gameMeta.ts only. What does the player see?

A fallback cover in the catalogue, a working setup page, and on play a blank black screen: GameContainer falls through to the canvas branch and GameRenderer logs "Unknown game".

Why does a missed question not show its answer straight away?

It is queued to come back. The feedback carries willRetry, the container passes an insight to QuestionCard, and the card hides the right choice.
